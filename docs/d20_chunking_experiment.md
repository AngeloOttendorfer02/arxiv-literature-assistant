# D-20 Chunking experiment

Pool: the 1,000 D-12 full-text papers, cleaned and chunked like the database build (D-10, D-11, D-13). Embeddings: `ibm-granite/granite-embedding-english-r2@47ea694b257b703fee9253d75c2b1f2985180498`, fp32, exact cosine search on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Ti SUPER. Retrieval: dense and BM25 (k1 = 1.2, b = 0.75, Python implementation, not pg_search), fused with RRF k = 60. Seed 1789. Generated by `scripts/chunk_experiment.py`.

## Benchmarks

- **A, LitSearch (S1 topic search):** 597 of 597 queries have at least one target paper in the pool; targets outside the pool are ignored. Chunks of the whole pool are ranked and reduced to papers by their first chunk. Dev/test 50/50, stratified by `query_set` (300/297); a different split than D-22 (seed 42).
- **B, synthetic passage questions (D-19, O16):** 86 questions generated by `gpt-oss:20b` from 1 to 2 sentence passages, independent of chunk boundaries. A hit is a returned chunk that holds at least 50% of the source span. Hit@k ranks all chunks of the pool. S1 Hit@2 assumes the source paper is among the top papers and picks its 2 passages exactly like `SearchBackend.chunks` (question plus the fixed S1 terms, 10 dense and 10 BM25 candidates, RRF, `select_passages`). Hit@budget returns about 1024 tokens (k = 4, 3, 2 for 256, 384, 512).

## Decision rule (fixed before the run)

Primary metrics: LitSearch dev NDCG@10 and S1 Hit@2. A setting whose paired bootstrap interval (10,000 draws, 95 %) against the best setting includes 0 on both metrics counts as equivalent. Among equivalent settings the one with fewer chunks above the 2,400-character evidence limit wins, then the smaller size (lower NLI latency per sentence). Test values are reported only.

**Result: 256/10** (equivalent: 256/10, 512/0, 512/10, 512/20).
