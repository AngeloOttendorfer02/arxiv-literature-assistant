\section{Empirical Results and Performance Benchmarking}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We serve \texttt{qwen3.8:27b} in bf16, q8\_0 and q4\_K\_M with Ollama 0.40.5 on one A100 80\,GB (Modal) and answer all 597 LitSearch queries once per precision, each with the same S1 prompt from the app retrieval (median 3{,}000 tokens).
Citation recall and precision follow ALCE~\cite{gao2023alce}, scored with the app's NLI model; answers stating that the evidence holds nothing matching count as ``not found''.
Precisions are compared with bf16 by a paired bootstrap (95\,\%).

\paragraph{Results.}
Quantization cuts memory by a factor of three and speeds up decoding by 58\,\%, but doubles the engine TTFT, since prompt evaluation is compute-bound and decoding bandwidth-bound.
Decoding dominates the answer time, so q4\_K\_M is faster than bf16 on 522 of 597 queries.
Quantization costs no citation quality: q8\_0 matches bf16, and q4\_K\_M even scores about 3 points higher, because it writes shorter answers with fewer multi-source sentences, which are the hardest to entail.
The ``not found'' rate (0.16 to 0.17) does not differ significantly.

\paragraph{End to end.}
Through the whole app (q4\_K\_M, NLI on the GPU, D072), a single request takes 22 to 30\,s, of which the LLM answer is under half.
Before generation, the planner (3.6 to 6.2\,s) and Prompt Guard on the retrieved sources (6 to 6.5\,s, CPU) dominate; generation with the sentence checks takes 11 to 16\,s.

\paragraph{Trade-offs.}
We serve q4\_K\_M: a third of the memory of bf16, faster answers and no measurable quality loss.
Its 1.2\,s higher TTFT is small next to the guardrail and planning overhead, which is the price of checked, cited answers.
