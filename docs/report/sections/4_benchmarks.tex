\section{Empirical Results and Performance Benchmarking}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We serve \texttt{qwen3.8:27b} in bf16, q8\_0 and q4\_K\_M with Ollama 0.40.5 on one A100 80\,GB (Modal); the model has no fp16 tag, so bf16 is the 16-bit variant.
Each precision answers all 597 LitSearch queries once with the same S1 prompt built from the app's retrieval (median 3{,}000 tokens), one request at a time.
Citation recall and precision follow ALCE~\cite{gao2023alce}, scored with the app's NLI model; answers stating that the evidence holds nothing matching count as ``not found''.

\paragraph{Results.}
Quantization cuts memory by a factor of three and speeds up decoding by 58\,\%, but doubles the engine TTFT, since prompt evaluation is compute-bound and decoding bandwidth-bound.
Decoding dominates the answer time, so q4\_K\_M is faster than bf16 on 522 of 597 queries.
Quantization costs no citation quality: q8\_0 matches bf16, and q4\_K\_M scores about 3 points higher; its answers are shorter (median 187 against 213 tokens) with fewer multi-source sentences, which are the hardest to entail, so we do not read this as a quality gain.

\paragraph{End to end.}
Through the whole app (q4\_K\_M, 12 LitSearch questions, one at a time, guards as in Section~\ref{sec:guardrails}), a request takes 17.6\,s in the median and the first checked sentence appears after 13.7\,s.
Of this, the planner's model call takes 4.1\,s, retrieval 0.6\,s and generation including the sentence checks 12.1\,s.

\paragraph{Trade-offs.}
We serve q4\_K\_M: a third of the memory of bf16, faster answers and no measurable loss in citation quality.
Its 1.2\,s higher TTFT is small next to the planner call, and the guards add 2.4\,s per request (Table~\ref{tab:guardrails}).
That is the price of answers whose every sentence is backed by a cited source; it also removes faithful sentences that combine two sources (26\,\% FRR), which we accept, as removing a true sentence is safer than showing an unsupported one.
