\section{Architectural Trade-offs, Bottlenecks and Post-Mortem}
\label{sec:tradeoffs}

\paragraph{Bottlenecks under load.}
We sent 1, 2, 4 and 8 parallel requests through the whole app (q4\_K\_M, 12 different LitSearch questions per level, Ollama with 8 slots on the Modal A100), once with the sentence check on the laptop CPU and once on the GPU (Table~\ref{tab:load}).
On the CPU, throughput stayed at 1.1 to 1.6 requests per minute and 8 of 12 requests timed out at 8 parallel requests.
The GPU was not the limit: it decoded about 43 tokens/s per request, while the app consumed 4 to 6 tokens/s in total.
The bottleneck was the laptop CPU, which NLI and Prompt Guard on the sources shared; the check alone took 20\,s of a 38\,s request.
With NLI on the GPU all 48 requests finished and throughput rose from 1.9 to 5.9 requests per minute.
It flattens between 4 and 8 parallel requests (5.7 and 5.9), so the next limit is on the inference side; we did not measure which resource it is.
The database is no bottleneck: retrieval took 0.8 to 3.5\,s and the SQL stage under 2\,ms at every level.

\paragraph{Discarded approaches.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The small NLI model.}
We first chose EttinX-nli-s (68M) for the sentence check, because it needs 0.25\,s per pair on the CPU against 1.7\,s for ModernCE-large, and it was right on our 7 hand-made pairs.
On 1{,}034 pairs from 150 real abstracts it rejected 23\,\% of verbatim sentences with a 200-token premise and 87\,\% with a 600-token premise; ModernCE-base passed 87\,\% and 53\,\%, ModernCE-large 100\,\% and 99\,\%, while manipulated sentences were passed by at most 14\,\% in all three.
Lesson: seven examples say nothing about accuracy, and a model must be tested with the premise length it will see in production. The price of the larger model, six times the CPU latency, led to the next point.
\item \textbf{The sentence check on the application CPU.}
Keeping the check next to the API looked simple, and it was fast enough for a single request. Under load it was the first thing to break: the check took 20\,s of a 38\,s request while the GPU generated the answer in about 6\,s, throughput did not grow with parallel requests, and most requests timed out at 8.
We moved NLI and later Prompt Guard on the sources to the inference server (two-tier design), without changing models or thresholds.
Lesson: we had assumed the language model would be the bottleneck, but the safety layer was; only a test of the whole pipeline showed this, an engine benchmark alone would not have.
\end{enumerate}
