\section{Hybrid Indexing and Retrieval Pipeline}
\label{sec:retrieval}

\paragraph{Chunking.}
Full texts are available for 1{,}000 papers (all query targets, the demo topics and a random sample).
After removing front matter and bibliography, each numbered section is split into windows of a fixed number of tokens of the embedding tokenizer; consecutive windows overlap, and no chunk crosses a section boundary.
We compared all nine combinations of 256, 384 and 512 tokens with 0, 10 and 20\,\% overlap on two benchmarks: the 597 LitSearch queries at paper level, ranking chunks of the whole pool with the hybrid search, and 86 synthetic questions about passages of one or two sentences, drawn independently of chunk boundaries, generated by a local LLM, filtered for copied wording and reviewed by hand (8 of 100 rejected in a full pass, 22 of 25 agreement in a second reviewer's sample).
A hit counts when a returned chunk holds at least half of the source span; S1 Hit@2 reproduces the production step that picks two passages per paper.
Before the run we fixed the rule: settings not significantly worse than the best on LitSearch NDCG@10 (dev) and S1 Hit@2 (paired bootstrap, 95\,\%) are equivalent, and among them the one that is not truncated in the prompt and is cheaper to validate wins.
Table~\ref{tab:chunking} shows that quality barely depends on the setting, while cost does: 10\,\% of 512-token chunks exceed the 2{,}400-character limit per source, and NLI validation of a sentence against a full chunk takes about twice as long.
At an equal budget of about 1{,}024 tokens, 256-token chunks contain the source span in 84\,\% of the questions against 62\,\% for 512 tokens.
We therefore use 256 tokens with 10\,\% overlap (48{,}860 chunks); 384 tokens performed within noise of it.

\paragraph{Embedding and index.}
We compared three 768-dimensional base-size embedders on paper retrieval over title and abstract of all 56{,}115 papers with the 597 LitSearch queries (exact cosine search, 50/50 dev/test split stratified by query set).
\texttt{ibm-granite/granite-embedding-english-r2} (revision \texttt{47ea694}) reached NDCG@10 0.510 and Recall@20 0.695 over all queries, against 0.453/0.670 for the planned default \texttt{BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5} and 0.483/0.687 for \texttt{cadet-embed-base-v1}.
Its NDCG@10 gain over bge-base is significant on both splits (paired bootstrap, dev $+0.076$ [$+0.041$, $+0.112$], test $+0.037$ [$+0.004$, $+0.070$]), so it replaced bge-base for papers, chunks, queries and the topic gate; the schema stays unchanged.
Vectors are stored in pgvector with an HNSW index (cosine, $m=16$, $ef_\mathrm{construction}=64$, $ef_\mathrm{search}=100$, iterative scan so that filtered searches stay complete).
BM25 runs in PostgreSQL via ParadeDB pg\_search on title (weight 0.5) and abstract, and on chunk text; filters on year, author and paper are part of the same SQL query.
Dense and BM25 lists are fused with reciprocal rank fusion ($k=60$).
On the full database, LitSearch Recall@20 is 0.690 dense, 0.607 BM25 and 0.704 hybrid.
