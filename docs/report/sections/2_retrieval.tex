\section{Hybrid Indexing and Retrieval Pipeline}
\label{sec:retrieval}

\paragraph{Data.}
We use LitSearch~\cite{ajith2024litsearch} (revision \texttt{9573fb2}): 597 literature-search queries over 64{,}183 papers with Semantic Scholar metadata and S2ORC full texts.
Quality filters (empty title, no author names, no section heading) leave 56{,}115 papers and keep all 574 query targets; the database holds 328{,}622 distinct citation pairs (Table~\ref{tab:data} in the appendix).
\paragraph{Chunking.}
Full texts are chunked for 1{,}000 papers (all query targets, the demo topics and a random sample); without front matter and bibliography, each section is split into windows of the embedding tokenizer with overlap; no chunk crosses a section boundary.
We compared 256, 384 and 512 tokens with 0, 10 and 20\,\% overlap on two benchmarks over this 1{,}000-paper pool: the LitSearch queries, and 86 synthetic questions about one- or two-sentence passages, generated by a local LLM and reviewed by hand (Table~\ref{tab:chunking} in the appendix).Quality barely depends on the setting (NDCG@10 0.77 to 0.80 on the pool), cost does: at an equal budget of about 1{,}024 tokens, 256-token chunks contain the source passage in 84\,\% of the questions against 62\,\% for 512 tokens, 10\,\% of 512-token chunks exceed the per-source prompt limit, and NLI on a full chunk takes about twice as long.
We use 256 tokens with 10\,\% overlap (48{,}860 chunks; S1 Hit@2 0.76, Hit@5 0.87).

\paragraph{Embedding and index.}
On paper retrieval over title and abstract of all 56{,}115 papers (597 queries, exact cosine search), \texttt{ibm-granite/granite-embedding-english-r2} reached NDCG@10 0.510 against 0.453 for the planned \texttt{BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5}; the gain is significant on both dev and test halves (paired bootstrap), so granite (768 dimensions) embeds papers, chunks, queries and the topic gate.
Vectors are stored in pgvector with an HNSW index (cosine, $m=16$, $ef_\mathrm{construction}=64$, $ef_\mathrm{search}=100$, iterative scan for filtered searches).
BM25 runs in PostgreSQL via ParadeDB pg\_search on title and abstract and on chunk text; year, author and paper filters are part of the same SQL query, and both lists are fused with reciprocal rank fusion ($k=60$).
In the production database, LitSearch Recall@20 is 0.690 dense (HNSW), 0.607 BM25 and 0.709 hybrid.

\paragraph{Foundation model.}
We use the open-weight Qwen~3.8 with 27B parameters (\texttt{qwen3.8:27b}~\cite{ollama_qwen38}); the demo serves its 4-bit variant \texttt{qwen3.8:27b-q4\_K\_M} with Ollama.
It was chosen because Ollama offers it in 16-, 8- and 4-bit variants, so the precision comparison of Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} runs in one engine, and at 27B the 16-bit variant (56\,GB) still fits one A100 80\,GB with room for the KV cache.
